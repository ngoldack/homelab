Write a short fictional short story of roughly 500-600 words in the style of a literary
novel. The story must have a clear arc: an opening that establishes a mood and a
character, a middle that introduces a quiet conflict, and a closing that resolves the
conflict without moralizing. Set it in a small coastal town during a long, slow autumn.
Give the protagonist a specific occupation (for example a lighthouse keeper, a
boatwright, or a mapmaker) and let that occupation shape how they perceive the
conflict. Use concrete sensory detail: salt air, the sound of halyards, the particular
quality of light in late afternoon. Vary sentence length deliberately, mixing short
declarative sentences with longer, more discursive ones. Avoid cliches about the sea.
Do not summarize the theme at the end; let the final image carry the meaning. Aim for
elegant, restrained prose with a precise vocabulary.
